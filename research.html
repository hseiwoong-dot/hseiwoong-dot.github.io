Job Market Paper

Build On or Branch Out? Patent Grants and Peers’ Innovation Policies
PDF
Abstract: When a firm is granted a patent, its technologically related peers can gain from the disclosed knowledge or lose from the patentee’s stronger exclusion rights. Instrumenting grants with patent examiner leniency, I find that granting a patent on an important innovation raises R&D expenses among technologically close peers and shifts the patenting of close product-market rivals toward new technologies. Stock market reactions on the grant date reveal which force dominates for a given peer. When the stock prices of both the patentee and the peer rise, the peer raises R&D expenses by about 7% and its acquisition probability by 3 percentage points and builds on the granted patent’s technology; when the peer’s stock price falls, it moves its patenting away from that technology. Financial flexibility amplifies these responses, and patent enforcement limits expansion.

Working Papers

Funding the Uncommon: Financial Incentives and Corporate Investment in Rare Disease Innovation
PDF
Abstract: Can financial incentives redirect corporate innovation toward socially valuable projects with limited commercial markets? I study the Rare Pediatric Disease Priority Review Voucher program, which provides a transferable financial reward for successful drug development. Using clinical-trial data and a difference-in-differences design, I find that the program increases public-firm participation in eligible drug development, including entry and continued investment in existing projects. The response primarily reflects an expansion of firms’ innovation portfolios rather than substitution away from other projects. These findings show that outcome-contingent financial incentives can affect both the level and direction of corporate innovation.
